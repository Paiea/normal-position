# The Comb

They argued about whether to test anything for eleven minutes.

That was better than Malia expected.

"No," Kalei said first.

Nalani said, "Why?"

"Because now we're doing what?"

"Checking."

"We've been checking."

"Not like this."

Malia sat at the main table with both hands flat.

She had not touched the pencil.

That felt important.

Keoni stood near the doorway, still in work clothes.

Koa was at school.

The house was quiet enough for everyone to hear when nobody answered immediately.

Nalani looked at Malia.

"Explain it again."

Malia did.

"No secret test."

Kalei crossed her arms.

"Good."

"No using Koa's stuff. No moving things people are actively using. No waiting to see who fixes something."

Nalani nodded.

Keoni said nothing.

Malia continued.

"We choose one object with a clear normal position and clear ownership. We all know it's being moved."

"Comb," Nalani said.

Kalei looked at her.

Nalani shrugged.

"We already know where it belongs."

Malia looked at Kalei.

Kalei looked away.

The comb sat in Tūtū Lehua's room.

Dark horn.

Wide teeth.

One end tooth worn shorter.

Shallow ceramic dish by the washstand.

Normal since before Malia came home.

Normal long before that.

Kalei said, "I don't like using her things like bait."

Nalani's face changed.

"She's dead."

"I know."

"Then what are you protecting?"

Kalei looked at her.

Malia felt the old argument opening.

She cut across it.

"Not bait."

Both sisters looked at her.

"If nothing happens, we put it back."

Nalani said, "And if something does?"

Malia looked toward the hallway.

"I don't know."

Keoni finally spoke.

"That's the part I hate."

Malia looked at him.

"Which part?"

"You're doing a test without knowing what a positive result means."

"It's not a lab."

"Exactly."

Nalani sat.

"So we don't do anything?"

Keoni shrugged.

"I didn't say that."

Kalei looked at him.

"You think we should?"

"No."

"Helpful."

"I think if we're going to do it, don't make it complicated."

Malia nodded.

"Agreed."

"No tape. No marks. No weird boxes."

Nalani looked at Malia.

Malia raised both hands.

"I wasn't going to."

Keoni stared.

"You were thinking about it."

"Maybe."

"Don't."

"Fine."

Kalei sat too.

Nobody looked comfortable.

That helped.

Comfort would have meant they were pretending to know what they were doing.

Nalani said, "I have to go into town anyway."

"For what?" Malia asked.

"Bank. Utility thing. Maybe pharmacy."

"Maybe?"

"If the line is stupid, no pharmacy."

Keoni nodded.

"Take it."

Kalei looked at him.

He lifted one shoulder.

"If she's off property with it, that's enough."

Malia said, "We see her leave with it."

"We see it leave," Keoni corrected.

"Same."

"No."

Malia waited.

Keoni looked at Nalani.

"You don't hand it to anybody."

"I wasn't planning to."

"You don't put the purse down somewhere and walk away."

Nalani stared at him.

"Do you think I'm five?"

"No. I think Malia will ask later."

Malia said, "True."

Nalani sighed.

"Fine."

Kalei still looked unhappy.

Malia said, "You can say no."

Kalei looked at her.

"Can I?"

"Yes."

That answer mattered enough to make the room quiet.

Kalei looked toward Tūtū Lehua's room.

Then back.

"Do it."

No one moved right away.

Nalani stood first.

"Before I change my mind."

They went together.

Tūtū Lehua's room was bright from the east window.

The bed remained stripped down to ordinary clean bedding.

The top dresser drawer was closed.

The slippers were gone.

The comb sat in its dish.

Malia stopped at the washstand.

Nobody touched it.

Keoni leaned against the doorframe.

Kalei stood nearest the bed.

Nalani opened her purse.

It was black nylon.

Cheap enough that one corner had started to fuzz.

She opened the main zipper.

Then a smaller inner zipper.

"Here?"

Malia said, "You choose."

Nalani stared at her.

"That was suspiciously healthy."

"I'm trying."

"Stop. It's weird."

Malia smiled despite herself.

Nalani held the inner pocket open.

Kalei picked up the comb.

Her hand knew it.

That was obvious.

Thumb at the spine.

Fingers away from the teeth.

She looked at it once.

Not dramatically.

Just long enough.

Then handed it to Nalani.

Nalani put it into the inner pocket.

Zipped the pocket.

Zipped the purse.

Held the purse up.

"Happy?"

"No," Malia said.

"Good."

Keoni walked over to the washstand.

The ceramic dish was empty.

He touched it with one finger.

Then looked underneath.

Malia almost asked what he expected.

Did not.

He straightened.

"Empty."

Kalei said, "We can all see that."

"I know."

Nalani slung the purse over her shoulder.

"Anything else?"

Malia looked at the dish.

"No."

Kalei said, "Go."

Nalani left.

They followed her to the main room.

Then the front door.

Then the lanai.

Malia hated that.

It felt theatrical.

Nalani noticed.

"You all coming to town too?"

"No."

"Then stop escorting me."

Keoni laughed.

Nalani crossed the yard.

Went through the gate.

Got into the car.

Purse still on her shoulder until she sat.

She put it on the passenger seat.

Malia saw.

Nalani saw Malia seeing.

She rolled down the window.

"Seriously?"

"Go."

Nalani gave her the finger.

Then drove away.

Kalei turned immediately.

"Now what?"

Malia looked at Keoni.

Keoni said, "Nothing."

"That's the plan?"

"Best one."

He went back to the workshop.

Kalei went to the cookhouse.

Malia stood in the yard.

Nothing.

Good.

She went inside.

At nine fourteen, Malia checked the dish.

Empty.

She did not call anyone.

At nine twenty-seven, Kalei checked.

Malia saw her do it.

Kalei saw Malia seeing.

Neither spoke.

At nine forty, Keoni came inside for water.

Malia said, "Check."

He stared at her.

"Please."

He went down the hall.

Returned.

"Empty."

"What did you touch?"

"Nothing."

"Did you look under the dish?"

Keoni looked at her.

Malia sighed.

"Sorry."

"You're bad at this."

"I know."

"Good."

Did not answer.

Then answered:

Okay.

Deleted a second message before sending it.

At ten twelve, Kalei made coffee.

At ten nineteen, a delivery driver came to the gate with a small package for Keoni.

At ten twenty-three, Koa texted Kalei asking if he could go to Micah's after school.

At ten thirty-one, nothing happened.

Malia checked.

Empty dish.

At ten forty-six, she stopped checking.

Not because she had become disciplined.

Because Aunty Nani arrived.

She came through the gate carrying a bag of limes and complaining about somebody's dog.

Malia did not know whose dog.

Possibly nobody's.

Aunty Nani had the ability to make community information sound like a personal grievance even when it did not involve her.

Kalei took the limes.

"Why?"

"Tree's stupid."

"Too much?"

"Too much."

"Keoni wants some."

"Then he can have."

Aunty Nani sat.

Malia listened to fifteen minutes about limes, dogs, a broken freezer, and somebody's nephew who had bought the wrong truck.

It was almost soothing.

At eleven ten, Aunty Nani left.

At eleven twelve, Malia realized she had not thought about the comb for twenty-six minutes.

She felt proud.

Then immediately thought about the comb.

Less impressive.

Kalei was washing limes at the cookhouse sink.

Malia went to help.

"Don't."

Malia stopped.

"What?"

"You'll stare at the house from here."

"I can wash limes."

"You can obsess from anywhere."

"Also true."

Kalei handed her a towel.

"Dry."

Malia dried.

They worked.

No conversation about Lehua.

No conversation about Nohea.

No conversation about what might be moving through the family place.

Just limes.

At eleven thirty-eight, Keoni came in.

He picked one up.

Kalei slapped his hand.

"Wash."

"It's getting peeled."

"Wash."

He washed it.

Malia watched him.

Keoni noticed.

"What?"

"Nothing."

He narrowed his eyes.

Malia corrected herself.

"I'm not asking."

"Good."

He took the lime outside.

At eleven fifty, Nalani texted again.

Utility done. Pharmacy line stupid. Leaving.

Malia looked at the message.

Her chest loosened.

Nothing happened.

Good.

The experiment had failed.

That was good.

She believed that for almost three minutes.

Then Kalei said, "Malia."

Not loud.

Not frightened.

Just her name.

Malia looked up.

Kalei stood at the cookhouse door facing the main house.

"What?"

Kalei did not answer.

She walked.

Malia followed.

They crossed the yard using the west route.

The old diagonal remained clear enough to use.

Neither took it.

Main house.

Hall.

Tūtū Lehua's room.

Kalei stopped in the doorway.

Malia saw the comb.

It lay in the ceramic dish.

Dark horn.

Wide teeth.

One end tooth shorter.

Normal.

Exactly normal.

Malia did not enter.

Her body stopped before thought did.

Kalei said, "I didn't."

Malia looked at her.

Kalei's face was pale.

Not movie pale.

Just less color around the mouth.

"I know."

"Do you?"

"Yes."

"How?"

"I was with you."

"Not all morning."

Malia looked at the comb.

The room was ordinary.

Window open.

Bed still.

No drawer moved.

No cloth disturbed.

Nothing else changed.

Malia said, "Don't touch it."

Kalei laughed once.

Sharp.

"Wasn't planning to."

Malia backed away.

"Keoni."

He was in the yard.

She did not shout.

She walked to the front door.

"Keoni."

Something in her voice brought him immediately.

"What?"

Malia pointed down the hall.

He looked at her.

Then at Kalei.

Then went to the room.

Not close enough to touch.

Looked at the dish.

Then under the washstand.

Then behind it.

Then under the bed.

"What are you checking?"

"Everything I can."

He crouched.

Looked at the floorboards.

Window latch.

Screen.

"Window been open?"

Kalei said, "All morning."

"Screen stays?"

"Yeah."

He checked the frame.

Nothing.

Then the comb.

Still no touch.

"Same comb?"

Malia said, "Short tooth."

Kalei said, "Same."

Keoni looked at the worn tooth.

Then the spine.

"Any mark?"

Kalei shook her head.

"No."

"Crack?"

"Small scratch near the end."

He leaned.

Malia knew the scratch.

Faint pale line near the spine where the horn had scuffed years ago.

It was there.

Keoni stood.

"Call Nalani."

She called.

"What?"

"Pull over."

"What happened?"

"Where's the comb?"

"Mom."

"In my purse."

Longer silence.

He did not move.

"What does no mean?"

"Mom."

"It's not here."

"Yes."

"Yes."

"No."

"I'm going to sit here."

"Okay."

"I'll call."

"Don't."

Good.
