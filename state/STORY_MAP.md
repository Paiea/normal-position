# NORMAL POSITION — STORY MAP

Status: ACTIVE WORKING SPINE.
31 chapters.
Future contracts may mutate while preserving governing arc.

### 01 — The Hook
Malia returns to Salen for Lehua's Keeping Days.
Establish front key, Malia's reset habit, family place labor, family friction.
No confirmed supernatural event.

Exit:
Malia automatically returns something to place and realizes Lehua taught her the motion.

### 02 — Keeping Days
Visitors, food, cleanup, estate pressure.
Independent lives visible.
Lehua's room remains practical, not shrine-like.

Exit:
Malia notices one ordinary object where she would not have put it.
Human explanation easy.

### 03 — Current Normal
Lio correctly identifies that an adult's remembered "usual place" is outdated.
Establish generational conflict over normal position.

Exit:
reader learns current normal can differ from inherited normal.

### 04 — The Wrong Cup
A cup / shelf arrangement appears in an older configuration.
Nalani reacts too strongly.

Exit:
Nohea's name almost enters, then does not.

### 05 — Lehua's Room
Privacy, inheritance, and caregiver access collide.
Kalei has legitimate reasons to touch nearly everything.
Malia's universal-reset instinct is challenged.

Exit:
one private object moved for a clearly human reason.

### 06 — Back on the Hook
Front key appears on hook after a confusing custody gap.
Still fully human-plausible.

Exit:
Malia starts tracking rather than resetting immediately.

### 07 — The Old Shelf
An arrangement corresponds to Malia's childhood rather than current house use.
Nohea is named plainly.

Exit:
first strong evidence that "wrong" may mean "older."

## ACT II — CONTAMINATION

### 08 — Throw It Away
Nalani removes Tūtū Lehua's worn house slippers for ordinary estate / cleanup reasons.
Kalei resists emotionally but does not forbid it.
Malia catches herself waiting for the slippers to come back.

Exit:
they stay gone.

Purpose:
negative result.
The house does not perform on demand.

### 09 — Nothing Happened
Keoni's off-plot repair / family obligations dominate.
Malia checks the empty slipper place and another established object state.

Nothing changes.

Exit:
Malia feels embarrassed by how much attention she has given absence.

Purpose:
real false negative and lived time.

### 10 — Keoni's Tools
Malia misreads Keoni's workshop as disorder.
Keoni demonstrates a functional normal she cannot see.

A moved tool / part proves somebody entered the workspace, but Keoni identifies the mundane cause through expertise Malia lacks.

Exit:
Malia accepts that "normal position" depends on whose work the object belongs to.

Purpose:
limit protagonist monopoly before the preference phase.

### 11 — The Old Path
Ordinary errands, drying, food movement, tools, and household traffic accidentally leave a chain of objects / clearings that reconstruct an older route between the main house and pre-rebuild cookhouse.

Every placement has a plausible human cause.

No one person created the route.

Exit:
Malia can walk the obsolete path without using imagination to fill gaps.

Purpose:
first spatial escalation beyond shelves.

### 12 — The Clear Way
Malia deliberately restores the present-day yard / cookhouse flow.

Over the day, different people independently move ordinary things for practical reasons:
a chair, laundry basket, hose, cooler, tools, shoes, folding table.

By evening, the current route is awkward again while the older route is conspicuously clear.

Nobody intended that outcome.

Exit:
Koa chooses the old route automatically because it is simply the easiest way through.

Purpose:
the place begins to feel as if it has a preference while every movement remains human.

### 13 — Nohea's Place
Household crowding forces temporary rearrangement inside the main house.

Malia and Nalani independently move different objects to "make room."
Kalei later recognizes that together they have reconstructed the spatial footprint of Nohea's childhood place:
sleeping edge, cup side, small storage position, chair orientation.

Neither woman consciously remembered doing that.

Each movement is explainable.
The aggregate is not comfortable.

Exit:
Malia realizes the old arrangement can be recreated by people who are actively trying to use the present house.

Purpose:
old normal begins recruiting living behavior without proving supernatural compulsion.

### 14 — Same Answer
Malia tracks several unrelated household decisions across one day without manipulating them.

Different people solve different practical problems:
- where to set something down
- which route to leave open
- where to move a chair
- which shelf to free
- where to stage rain gear

Again and again, the practical answer converges on the same older household grammar.

Nalani and Kalei remember different eras, but both recognize that the pattern is no longer random enough to dismiss comfortably.

Keoni remains cautious:
human choices still explain every individual event.

Exit:
Malia stops asking, "Who moved this?"
She asks:
**Why do we keep choosing the same old answer?**

Purpose:
secure the genre before confirmation.
The reader should feel preference, not proof.

### 15 — The Comb
Tūtū Lehua's comb is deliberately removed from the family place under observed custody.

Its absence is verified.

It later returns to its normal ceramic dish beside the washstand under conditions that close the ordinary handoff paths established earlier.

Exit:
first clean supernatural confirmation.

The uncertainty does not become "what is happening?"
It becomes:
**what exactly has been choosing?**

### 27 — What Leaves the House

### 28 — Open House

Exit:

### 29 — The Storm Door

### 30 — No One's Place

- move ownership
- change sleeping
- open / close different paths
- dismantle one inherited placement system
- keep useful storm procedure without obeying family assignment

No banishment speech.

Exit:
family place stops correcting them long enough to survive night.

### 31 — Morning Inventory

Final object:

Malia notices.
