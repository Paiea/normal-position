# NORMAL POSITION — FAMILY HISTORY LOCK

Status: LOCKED ENOUGH FOR BOOK ONE DRAFTING.

This file contains author knowledge.
Characters do not automatically know all of it.

## The Nohea storm

Twenty-four years before Chapter One:

- Malia is 10.
- Nohea Kealoha, Malia's younger sister, is 7.
- A severe island storm is already underway.
- Lehua runs the household's storm procedure with extreme precision.
- Nalani and her daughters are staying at the family place.
- Kalei and young Keoni are also present.

### The immediate problem

Earlier that day, Nohea hides a small personal item in the detached cookhouse after an argument with Malia.

Working object:
a cheap painted tin whistle.

The whistle itself is NOT supernatural and must not become a cursed-object solution.

After the main house is secured for the worst weather, Nohea slips out during a temporary lull to retrieve it.

She does not tell Malia.

### Lehua's choice

Lehua realizes Nohea is outside before the fatal moment.

Opening the main storm door during the strongest gust cycle creates a real risk:
- pressure on the door
- flying debris
- loss of the secure interior
- danger to everyone clustered inside

Lehua chooses to wait for the next lull rather than open immediately.

This choice is not obviously irrational.

It is also not cleanly defensible.

The detached kitchen structure partially fails before the lull.

Nohea is fatally injured.

### What Malia remembers

Malia remembers:
- Nohea missing
- adults shouting
- the storm
- her mother trying to move toward the door
- Lehua stopping somebody
- the sound of something striking the outer wall
- later being told Nohea had slipped out and nobody knew until too late

Malia does NOT remember hearing Lehua say she knew Nohea was outside.

She was a child under extreme stress.

Do not manufacture repressed-memory melodrama.
Her memory is incomplete, not magically blocked.

### What Nalani knows

Nalani knows Lehua understood Nohea was outside before the kitchen failed.

Nalani tried to open the door.

Lehua stopped her.

Nalani has spent decades treating leaving Hawaiʻi Island as the only morally survivable response.

She has never forgiven Lehua.

She has also never told Malia the full sequence because:
- Malia was a child
- Nalani feared turning Malia's grief into hatred
- Nalani did not want to relive her own failure to overpower Lehua
- silence became easier each year

### What Kalei knows

Kalei also knows.

Her interpretation differs.

She believes opening at that exact moment might have killed more people.

She does not think Lehua was right in some abstract moral sense.

She thinks Lehua made an impossible storm decision and then carried it badly.

Kalei's loyalty to Lehua includes anger at Nalani for converting one terrible choice into a total judgment of their mother.

This disagreement is real.

The book must not announce a single morally correct reading.

### What Keoni knows

Keoni was 13.

He knows more than Malia and less than Nalani / Kalei.

He remembers:
- Lehua controlling the door
- Nalani fighting her
- the timing of the gusts
- helping after the kitchen failed

His later mechanical / weather competence changes how he interprets the risk.

He is NOT automatic proof that Kalei is right.

### What Lehua did afterward

Lehua simplified the family story.

She allowed:
"Nohea slipped outside in the storm and we realized too late."

She did not publicly confess:
"I knew she was outside and chose to wait."

After Nohea's death, Lehua aggressively reorganized the family place:
- Nohea's sleeping place removed
- child belongings boxed
- cup reassigned / removed
- routes and chores changed
- cookhouse rebuilt / repaired
- storm rules tightened
- normal positions standardized even more strongly

This is when Malia's childhood training in exact placement became especially intense.

### Why the haunting reaches backward

The supernatural pattern is NOT "Nohea wants revenge."

The family place's returning arrangements gradually reach toward the last deeply repeated household configuration in which Nohea still occupied a place.

Lehua's later decades of forced order sit on top of that older layer.

This produces conflicting normals.

The horror is partly that the house has no ethical distinction between:
- care
- grief
- surveillance
- routine
- assigned role

It remembers arrangement.

## Present-tense mirror

Kalei must eventually make a current storm / access decision that echoes Lehua without duplicating it.

Locked function:
Kalei restricts another family member's movement because she believes immediate freedom creates greater collective danger.

Malia understands the reasoning.

Malia still rejects the unilateral choice.

Kalei does not immediately retract it or apologize it away.

Exact scene remains flexible until Act III rehearsal.
