# NORMAL POSITION — PROJECT STATE

Status: DRAFTING / CHAPTER 11 PUBLISHED

Working title: **Normal Position**

## North star

A Native Hawaiian family regathers at a multigenerational family place on windward Hawaiʻi Island after the death of its matriarch.

Malia Kealoha, 34, notices where things belong for a living. She supervises housekeeping at a Waikīkī hotel and is unusually good at seeing when a room has been used, reset badly, or altered by inches.

At her grandmother's house, ordinary objects begin returning to older positions:
- a key back on its hook
- a chair facing an old wall
- a cup returned to a shelf nobody uses
- a comb reappearing after being thrown away
- a child's place being quietly reconstructed

The house is not merely putting things away.

It is restoring a version of the family.

The horror question is:

**Whose version of normal is coming back, and what happens when people are treated like household objects with assigned places?**

## POV

Close third person centered on **Malia Kealoha**.

Do not use multi-POV unless a later structural audit proves it necessary.

## Protagonist

Malia Kealoha, 34.

Profession:
housekeeping operations supervisor at a large off-island hotel.

Competence:
- notices placement and residue
- understands reset routines
- reads a room through practical use
- can tell what changed without always knowing why
- trains others to produce repeatable normal states

Strength under pressure:
**restore order.**

Danger:
she can mistake restoration for care.

Her arc is not "learn to tolerate mess."

It is:
**learn that normal can be an imposed history, and that preserving a system may preserve the violence inside it.**

## Setting

Contemporary Hawaiʻi.

Malia currently lives on Oʻahu for work.

Primary location:
the Kealoha family place on windward Hawaiʻi Island, north of Hilo.

The property is contemporary and multigenerational, not a museum reconstruction of a traditional kauhale.

Working layout:
1. older main family house
2. detached cookhouse / outside kitchen
3. workshop / boat-and-tool shed
4. covered lanai / yard
5. rain-catchment system
6. gate / road access

The functional separation echoes the broader Hawaiian logic of household spaces serving different work, while the present buildings reflect generations of rebuilding and adaptation.

Culture authority:
see CULTURAL_SUBSTRATE.md.

Do not use the ʻāina, Hawaiian culture, or ancestral identity itself as the source of evil.

## Dead matriarch

**Lehua Kealoha**, 81.

Recently dead before Chapter One.

Malia learned many of her placement habits from Lehua.

Lehua was:
- observant
- exacting
- useful
- controlling
- capable of care
- capable of using order as surveillance

Do not reduce her to saint or monster.

Her household logic should remain useful enough that characters understand why it survived.

## Buried family wound

Nohea's death is the family's oldest unresolved fracture.

The public family version is incomplete.

Do not turn the entire book into a whodunit about Nohea.

The horror should use the old wound to pressure current relationships, object history, and definitions of care.

## Supernatural working rules

LOCKED AT CONCEPT LEVEL:

- the house does not create objects
- it moves or restores existing household objects
- early activity occurs when unwatched or unregistered
- movement follows recognizable prior arrangements rather than random haunting
- different eras of "normal" may conflict
- repeated human routines matter
- the force can exploit architecture and object placement before it can meaningfully coerce bodies
- exact cosmology may remain unresolved

NOT YET LOCKED:
- whether one consciousness causes the phenomenon
- whether Lehua understood it fully
- whether the house is memory, spirit, place, family pattern, or something less nameable
- exact limit on how far an object may move
- exact condition under which bodily coercion begins

Do not solve these through late exposition.

## Ending target

The climax should not destroy an evil object.

The family must change the household's lived pattern.

Preferred ending logic:
- redistribute possession
- change routes
- open or repurpose rooms
- refuse inherited assignments
- establish new shared routines
- accept that maintenance is ongoing

The final beat may preserve one unresolved object deviation.

Survival creates work.
