# NORMAL POSITION — CHARACTER CARDS

Status: WORKING. Lock after auditions.

## Malia Kealoha — 34

ROLE:
Primary POV. Housekeeping operations supervisor off-island.

PUBLIC:
Competent, controlled, useful, dry.

PRIVATE WANT:
Get through the the days around Tūtū Lehua's service, settle the estate, and leave without becoming a daughter of the house again.

STRENGTH:
Can see when a room has changed.

EXPENSIVE VERSION:
Assumes returning a system to standard is inherently helpful.

BODY:
Resets while talking.
Squares objects without noticing.
Checks surfaces by touch.
Uses work to avoid emotional exposure.

VOICE:
Direct but not theatrical.
Operational vocabulary under stress.
Asks what changed before asking what it meant.

PRIVATE INTERPRETATION:
Fast comparison loops.
Can obsess over small physical discrepancies to avoid larger emotional uncertainty.

OBJECT RELATION:
Returns things.
Labels mentally.
Hates ambiguous ownership.

BORING OFF-PLOT LIFE:
Staff scheduling, linen loss rates, training new supervisors, complaint logs.

## Nalani Kealoha — 58

ROLE:
Malia's mother. Lehua's younger daughter.

PUBLIC:
Practical, impatient with island nostalgia, wants the estate resolved.

PRIVATE WANT:
Leave before the house makes her feel like a daughter again.

STRENGTH:
Can sever ties and act.

EXPENSIVE VERSION:
Calls avoidance freedom even when other people pay for her disappearance.

BODY:
Does not settle into rooms.
Keeps bags packed.
Uses other people's cups rather than choosing one.

VOICE:
Changes subject through logistics.
Uses humor to reduce emotional scale.
Rarely says "I don't remember" when she can say "it doesn't matter."

OBJECT RELATION:
Travels light.
Throws away damaged things.
Dislikes inherited possessions.

## Kalei Kealoha — 62

ROLE:
Lehua's older daughter. Long-term caregiver. Stayed.

PUBLIC:
Capable, hospitable, exhausted, knows every household procedure.

PRIVATE WANT:
For the years she spent keeping the family place functioning to count as something more than being trapped.

STRENGTH:
Sustained care.

EXPENSIVE VERSION:
Believes care can justify access, monitoring, and unilateral decisions.

BODY:
Keeps hands busy.
Notices who leaves work unfinished.
Touches family belongings with assumed permission.

VOICE:
Concrete.
Remembers dates through tasks.
Can sound gentle while refusing to yield.

OBJECT RELATION:
Repairs, preserves, reassigns.
Knows where nearly everything belongs.

PRESENT-TENSE DANGER:
Must eventually make one protective / controlling choice Malia understands and still rejects.

## Keoni Kealoha — 37

ROLE:
Kalei's son. Malia's cousin. Boat and engine repair.

PUBLIC:
Busy, practical, not interested in turning every oddity into a theory.

PRIVATE WANT:
Keep his actual life from being swallowed by the returning family drama.

STRENGTH:
Understands material failure and weather.

EXPENSIVE VERSION:
Can dismiss emotional or supernatural claims until physical proof becomes unavoidable.

BODY:
Tests before theorizing.
Puts tools back by function, not beauty.
Leaves conversations to finish real work.

VOICE:
Longer when explaining mechanics.
Shorter when family gets abstract.
Does not argue for sport.

OBJECT RELATION:
Repairs.
Cannibalizes broken equipment.
Hates throwing away useful parts.

FRAME-BREAKER FUNCTION:
Has legitimate expertise Malia does not share.
His boring work must remain active.

## Koa Kealoha — 16

ROLE:
Keoni's son.

PUBLIC:
Restless, observant, somewhat amused by adult family drama.

PRIVATE WANT:
Be treated as someone who lives here, not a child visiting the adults' past.

STRENGTH:
Notices current household behavior because he has fewer nostalgic assumptions.

EXPENSIVE VERSION:
Experiments with things adults told him not to touch.

BODY:
Moves objects absentmindedly and therefore becomes useful baseline noise.

VOICE:
Specific, skeptical, less reverent about family stories.

OBJECT RELATION:
Uses things for what they do, not what they mean.

## Lehua Kealoha — deceased, 81

ROLE:
Dead matriarch whose systems remain materially present.

DO NOT:
make her ghost narrator, villain monologue, or all-knowing elder.

LIVED TRUTH:
She was genuinely good at keeping a multigenerational Hawaiʻi household functioning.

She also used order to know:
- who came home
- who touched what
- who lied
- who disobeyed
- what changed

Her care and control should be difficult to separate cleanly.

## Nohea Kealoha — died in childhood

ROLE:
Malia's younger sister.

Do not use her as a creepy dead-child aesthetic.

She needs:
- preferences
- annoyances
- bad habits
- object behavior
- relationships
- memory fragments that are not all about her death

Before Chapter 10, build a full childhood character card from surviving family evidence.
