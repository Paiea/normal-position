## Chapter 01 — The Hook

STATUS:
COMPLETE / PUBLISHED.

DRAMATIC JOB:
Establish Malia, family place, the days around Tūtū Lehua's service, family friction, and object-reading competence without overt haunting.

OPENING PRESSURE:
Malia arrives carrying travel fatigue and the expectation that she is temporary.

REQUIRED:
- front key normal position
- three-structure family place introduced through movement
- Kalei doing real work
- Keoni has off-plot obligation
- Koa current-life beat
- Nalani wants practical estate progress
- one Malia reset that feels useful
- Lehua remembered through task, not eulogy

FORBIDDEN:
- moving object with no human explanation
- spooky-child Nohea imagery
- supernatural exposition
- house personification

EXIT:
Malia catches herself performing one of Lehua's exact placement habits.

TARGET:
2,500-3,200 words.

## Chapter 03 — Current Normal

STATUS:
COMPLETE / PUBLISHED.

DRAMATIC JOB:
Prove normal position changes over time even without supernatural interference.

OPENING PRESSURE:
Malia begins treating remembered layout as reliable while helping with a practical household task.

OBJECT BASELINE:
use one object whose location changed during Lehua's later years for a legitimate reason.
Working candidate:
the spare storm lantern now kept in the main house rather than the gear house because Lehua's mobility declined.

HORROR MOVEMENT:
none required.
The discomfort comes from Malia being confidently wrong.

RELATIONSHIP MOVEMENT:
Koa correctly identifies current normal.
Malia must accept that he knows the present family place better than she does.
Do not turn Koa into a smug exposition device.

CULTURE MOVEMENT:
show how weather preparedness changes object placement and how current household needs can override inherited arrangement.

THREAD MOVEMENT:
T2 Malia as resetter
T5 Lehua's order
T7 Koa current normal
T9 weather

REQUIRED SETUP:
make explicit through action that an older remembered normal can be genuine and still no longer be correct.

FORBIDDEN:
- haunting confirmation
- treating Malia's memory as mysteriously corrupted
- making Koa's knowledge secretly supernatural

EXIT:
Malia holds two legitimate versions of where one thing belongs.

TARGET:
2,300-3,000 words.

- Nohea ghost implication

- dramatic horror language

2,400-3,100 words.

## Chapter 08 — Throw It Away

STATUS:
READY.

DRAMATIC JOB:
Test whether physically removing a Tūtū Lehua object causes restoration, and refuse to reward the expectation.

OPENING PRESSURE:
Nalani is clearing obvious unusable personal items from Lehua's room as estate work becomes less theoretical.

OBJECT:
Tūtū Lehua's old rubber house slippers.

BASELINE:
- clearly Lehua's
- worn smooth at heel
- one strap beginning to split
- normal late-life position beside her bedroom chair / bed when not worn
- no sacred or inherited significance

HUMAN ACTION:
Nalani decides they are worn-out personal items and throws them away / takes them out with actual trash.

RELATIONSHIP MOVEMENT:
- Kalei has a moment of resistance because the slippers are bound to caregiving routine
- Nalani argues that not every used thing needs preserving
- Malia recognizes she is waiting to see whether they come back
- nobody agrees to turn disposal into a supernatural experiment

CULTURE MOVEMENT:
Use the conflict to sharpen competing kuleana around:
- clearing
- maintaining
- preserving
- deciding what remains at a family place

Do not make "keeping old things" a Hawaiian trait.
This is this family.

HORROR MOVEMENT:
negative.
The slippers leave.
They stay gone.

THREAD MOVEMENT:
T2 Malia surveillance tendency
T3 Nalani / Kalei
T5 Lehua's order
T8 estate / family place
T10 human action

REQUIRED:
show the normal empty place after removal.

FORBIDDEN:
- slippers return
- substitute slippers appear
- noise / dream / omen
- comb involvement
- Nohea connection

EXIT:
the slippers remain gone.
Malia is disturbed by how strongly she expected otherwise.

TARGET:
2,500-3,200 words.
