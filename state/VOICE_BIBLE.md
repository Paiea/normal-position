# NORMAL POSITION — VOICE BIBLE

Status: ACTIVE.

## Narrative voice

Close third person through Mara.

Write for the ear.

No em dashes.

The prose should be:
- concrete
- spatial
- bodily
- quietly observant
- capable of dry humor
- increasingly pressured without becoming purple

The book should sound like somebody noticing a room too well.

## Mara's cognition

Her mind naturally compares:

expected / observed  
clean / used  
returned / left  
shared / personal  
open / latched  
current / older

Do not overuse one-word internal verdicts.

Avoid turning her into Lian with housekeeping vocabulary.

Her thoughts can:
- loop
- avoid grief through work
- jump from emotional pressure to a physical task
- notice irrelevant maintenance
- become petty
- misread people because objects feel easier

## Sensory hierarchy

Favor:
1. position
2. touch / residue
3. sound
4. practical smell
5. weather / air
6. visual description

Do not constantly describe darkness, shadows, cold spots, or uncanny silence.

## Horror language

Avoid early certainty words:
- haunted
- possessed
- evil
- cursed
- entity

Characters may say them.
Narration should earn them.

Avoid:
"The house wanted..."
unless it is clearly Mara's interpretation.

## Dialogue

Speaker ownership must survive audio.

Use said / asked when clean.

Use meaningful object handling instead of filler gestures.

Do not make everyone witty under pressure.

### Mara
operational questions, dry, impatient with vague claims.

### Celina
logistics, deflection, humor that reduces scale, abrupt exits.

### Inez
concrete household memory, gentle syntax with stubborn conclusions.

### Tomas
mechanical explanations become longer; family abstractions shorten him.

### Lio
specific, current, less reverent, willing to say adults are being weird.

## Nonverbal

High-value:
- returning an object
- failing to return it
- closing a shutter
- leaving a bag packed
- choosing another cup
- moving a chair
- not eating
- continuing repair
- taking a key
- changing which building someone sleeps in

Before explaining an emotional shift:
ask whether changed use can carry it.

## Repetition

Repeat phrases sparingly.

Prefer repeating actions whose meaning changes.

## Ear pass

After every chapter:
- identify every speaker without formatting
- break up ambiguous pronouns
- check thought vs speech distinction
- listen for repeated sentence skeletons
- cut explanation after physical beats
